When was the last time you actually did absolutely nothing? And no, sitting on the sofa watching a television show while simultaneously scrolling through videos of people chopping vegetables on your phone does not count.

We have completely murdered the blank space in our day.

Think about it. We used to have these tiny, beautiful pockets of absolute nothingness. Waiting for a kettle to boil meant staring at the kitchen tiles. Sitting on a bus meant looking out of a rainy window and pretending you were in a dramatic music video. Standing in a queue at the post office meant meditating on the back of a stranger's neck.

Now, if there is a three-second delay in our lives, the hand moves automatically. It is a biological reflex. We whip out the screen to check an app we closed four seconds ago, just to see if the internet managed to manufacture a new dopamine hit while we walked from the fridge to the toaster.

We have become completely incapable of being bored. But by curing our boredom, have we actually just ruined our brains?

## The Superpower of a Dull Sunday

We used to be absolute experts at being bored. If you grew up back when television only had four channels and most of them were showing snooker or farming documentaries, you knew the true weight of a dull afternoon. There was no internet to rescue you. There was no one to call because someone's mum was using the landline.

You were entirely stuck with yourself.

But that absolute boredom was actually a superpower. When the outside world offered zero entertainment, your brain had to build its own. We got lost in our imaginations. We invented stupid games, we stared at the ceiling until we saw patterns, and we actually let our minds wander down weird, creative rabbit holes.

Now, we never let the tank run dry. The second a hint of quiet creeps in, we plug ourselves right back into the grid. We are drowning in options, but we've completely starved our capacity to daydream. We've traded a limitless inner world for a five-inch piece of glass.

## The Takeaway

All the best ideas, the weird ones, the creative solutions, and the high-speed breakthroughs usually happen when the brain is so utterly desperate for entertainment that it has to start inventing things from scratch. By killing boredom, we might be killing our best ideas before they even start.

## Your Action for Today

We need to start rewilding our attention spans. Today, pick one mundane, everyday task and do it completely naked of technology.

When you go to boil the kettle, stand in line for your morning coffee, or wait for a mate to show up at the pub, leave your phone in your pocket. Do not check your notifications. Do not scroll a feed.

Just stand there. Look at the wall. Let your mind wander down whatever weird alleyway it chooses for exactly two minutes. Let yourself feel the friction of being completely unentertained, and see what your brain spits out when it realizes nobody is coming to rescue it.
