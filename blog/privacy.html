Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 June 2026

This site (blog.luketimms.online) is operated by Luke Timms as a personal blog. This policy explains what data is collected when you visit, why, and what your rights are under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.

Who controls your data

Luke Timms acts as the data controller. For privacy questions or to exercise your rights, contact lukedtimms@gmail.com.

What we collect

Currently: nothing personally identifying. The site is a static blog with no accounts, comments, or contact forms. Standard web-server access logs (IP address, timestamp, page requested) are recorded transiently for security and abuse-prevention; they are not used to build profiles and are rotated regularly.

If and when advertising is added (see "Third parties" below), additional data may be collected by ad providers under your consent.

Cookies

This site uses a small number of cookies and local-storage items:

  • Strictly necessary — a single value remembering your cookie-banner choice so we do not ask again on every visit. No personal data, no tracking.
  • Advertising and analytics (when enabled, not currently active) — third-party cookies set by ad networks such as Google AdSense. These are only loaded if you explicitly accept them via the cookie banner.

You can clear or block cookies at any time via your browser's settings.

Third parties

The site loads the following resources from third parties when you visit:

  • jsDelivr CDN — serves the markdown-rendering library used to display blog posts.

When advertising is added, additional third parties (e.g. Google AdSense) will be listed here and only loaded after consent.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you have the right to:

  • Access any personal data we hold about you
  • Correct inaccurate data
  • Have your data erased
  • Restrict or object to our use of your data
  • Data portability
  • Withdraw consent at any time (where consent is the basis for processing)

To exercise any of these rights, email lukedtimms@gmail.com. We aim to respond within 30 days.

Complaints

If you are unhappy with how your data has been handled, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office: ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date at the top will change too. Material changes (e.g. introducing advertising) will be flagged via the cookie banner.
